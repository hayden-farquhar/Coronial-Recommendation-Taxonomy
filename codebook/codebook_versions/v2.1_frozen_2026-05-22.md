# Coronial Recommendation Coding Guide — v2.1

**Status:** Working version, post v2.0 pilot. v2.1 is a **narrow mechanism-only revision** of v2.0. On the v2.0 pilot (n=44 clean-subset) four of the five axes hit Reliable — theme α=0.873, addressee α=0.812, scope α=0.872, specificity α=0.859 — and only mechanism (α=0.675) remained Tentative. v2.1 makes two changes to the mechanism axis and no changes to any other axis:

1. **Collapse `MECH-TRAIN` into `MECH-PROC`.** The training category was the persistent disagreement driver across v1.2 and v2.0 (TRAIN/PROC pairs accounted for the largest share of mechanism splits even after v2.0's decision tree sharpened the trigger). v2.0 was the less-aggressive structural fix (the decision tree); v2.1 is the more-aggressive fallback (the collapse), held in reserve in the v2.0 codebook and now applied.
2. **Promote the operational-endpoint tie-break for `MECH-INV` vs `MECH-PROC` to a top-level rule.** The v2.0 codebook had this rule embedded inside the decision-tree branches; v2.1 hoists it to its own block with the operational rule: **follow the verb chain to the last operational verb**. The recommendation's mechanism is the verb of the *endpoint*, not the verb of the *first step*.

No theme, addressee, specificity, or scope changes — those axes are already Reliable and the v2.0 codebook content for them is carried into v2.1 verbatim.

**Unit of analysis:** A single recommendation as extracted from a coronial finding. A finding with three numbered recommendations contributes three rows.

**Coding decision:** Each recommendation receives one label per axis (single-label, not multi-label). If a recommendation appears to legitimately span two categories on an axis, choose the *dominant* one and flag the case with the `ambiguous_<axis>` boolean for later review.

## Axis 1 — Theme

*What is the recommendation about?* Twelve mutually-exclusive categories. **No changes in v2.1.**

| Code | Label | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| THEME-MH | Mental health & suicide prevention | Screening, follow-up, crisis services, involuntary treatment, suicide prevention messaging. | "All EDs should have 24-hour psychiatric liaison." |
| THEME-CUST | Custody, detention, and police use of force | In-custody deaths (police, corrections, immigration); restraint techniques; Tasers; police-family interactions during fatal encounters. | "CCTV monitoring of all cells holding intoxicated detainees." "Review of Taser deployment protocols." |
| THEME-MED | Healthcare delivery | Clinical care, medication safety, hospital systems, ambulance services, vaccine information. | "Mandatory medication reconciliation on admission." |
| THEME-RTA | Road & transport safety | Road, rail, aviation, maritime *transport*. (Recreational water use is `THEME-REC`.) | "Lower speed limit on the Hume Highway between X and Y." |
| THEME-DV | Domestic & family violence | Intimate-partner violence response. | "Police to attend all reported breaches of ADVO." |
| THEME-DRUG | Substance use | Illicit drugs, alcohol, prescribing-related harm. | "Pill-testing pilots at music festivals." |
| THEME-CHILD | Child protection | Child welfare, out-of-home care, mandatory reporting. | "FACS to review case plan within 14 days of any reported absconding." |
| THEME-WORK | Workplace safety | Occupational health and safety. | "SafeWork NSW to issue guidance on confined-space entry." |
| THEME-REC | Recreational & water safety | Water sports, boating, swimming, river safety, drownings outside vehicles, beach safety, outdoor recreation. | "Develop public education programs about the dangers of inland river water-skiing." |
| THEME-FIRE | Fire & structural safety | Smoke detectors, building code, evacuation, residential fire risk, structural safety of dwellings. | "Smoke detectors should be mandatory in informal sleeping spaces." |
| THEME-PROD | Product, chemical & poison safety | Regulation of dangerous substances, consumer products, chemicals, poisons; product recalls. | "Restrict retail sale of concentrated potassium cyanide." |
| THEME-OTHER | Other / cross-cutting | Use only when none of the above fits. Flag for review. | (use sparingly) |

### Decision rules

- **Maritime transport (THEME-RTA) vs. recreational water (THEME-REC).** A commercial ferry sinking is RTA; a family in a tinnie capsizing while fishing is REC.
- **Custody (THEME-CUST) vs. mental health (THEME-MH).** If a death occurs in custody but the recommendation targets mental-health screening of detainees, prefer the *intervention* axis: a screening recommendation is MH, a cell-design recommendation is CUST.
- **Healthcare delivery (THEME-MED) vs. mental health (THEME-MH).** MED is for the *delivery system*; MH is for *psychiatric / suicide-specific* content. A general ED triage recommendation is MED; an ED psychiatric-liaison recommendation is MH.

## Axis 2 — Mechanism (decision tree, v2.1 — six categories)

*What kind of action is the recommendation calling for?* **Six mutually-exclusive categories** (down from seven in v2.0 — `MECH-TRAIN` collapsed into `MECH-PROC`).

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| MECH-LEG | Legislative / regulatory | Changing a law, regulation, or code of practice. |
| MECH-INFRA | Infrastructure / equipment | Installing, replacing, or supplying physical infrastructure or technology. |
| MECH-FUND | Funding / resourcing | Allocating money, FTE, or positions, where allocation is the only action. |
| MECH-INV | Investigation / inquiry | An audit, review, or further inquiry that is itself the **endpoint** of the recommendation. |
| MECH-PROC | Procedural | Policy, protocol, guideline, SOP, escalation pathway, **training programme, curriculum, or staff-education roll-out** — anything procedural that is not one of the above. |
| MECH-OTHER | Other / exhortation | Falls outside all of the above. Includes exhortations and observations without a specific named mechanism ("I urge X to take this seriously"). |

### Why MECH-TRAIN was collapsed into MECH-PROC

Across the v1.2 and v2.0 pilots, training-versus-procedure was the persistent disagreement driver on mechanism. v1.2's precedence rule named TRAIN > PROC when the operative verb was "train", but raters disagreed on what counted as "operative". v2.0 replaced the precedence rule with a decision tree whose Q3 sharpened the test ("the deliverable is the training itself, with or without an accompanying policy") and dropped the overall mechanism α from 0.585 to 0.675 — improvement, but still Tentative, and the two remaining TRAIN/PROC disagreements (out of ten total) on the v2.0 pilot were on policy-with-training rows where reasonable readers split on whether "training" or "the policy mandating it" was the deliverable.

The collapse is structural: training is now *one form of procedural change* alongside protocols, SOPs, and escalation pathways. A recommendation that "officers be trained in de-escalation" is now coded **MECH-PROC** — the deliverable is a staff-development programme, which is a category of procedural change. A recommendation that "the Police Force develop a de-escalation training curriculum" is also MECH-PROC. The boundary that previously separated TRAIN from PROC was not earning its α budget.

### Decision tree (Q1..Q6, v2.1)

Ask each question in order. **Take the first "yes". Do not re-evaluate later questions.**

1. **Q1 — Does the recommendation require amending a statute, regulation, or code of practice?**
 → **MECH-LEG**. Trigger phrases: "amend the [Act]", "the regulations should require", "introduce legislation", "regulation X should be replaced", "the [Act] should be amended to require".
2. **Q2 — Does the recommendation require installing, replacing, or supplying physical equipment or infrastructure?**
 → **MECH-INFRA**. Trigger phrases: "install [device]", "replace [equipment]", "fit out [facility]", "supply [device]", "purchase [equipment]", "provide [physical thing]", "all cells fitted with [device]".
3. **Q3 — Is the recommendation purely about allocating money, FTE, or positions, with no other operational action?**
 → **MECH-FUND**. Trigger phrases: "allocate [N] additional positions", "increase funding for", "[N] FTE", "fund [service]". If the recommendation also names a substantive policy / infrastructure change, that other change wins under Q1-Q2.
4. **Q4 — Is the recommendation an external audit, inquiry, or review **as an endpoint** (the deliverable is the review report itself, not a downstream change)?**
 → **MECH-INV**. Trigger phrases: "an audit should be undertaken of compliance with", "an independent review of [program] should be conducted", "refer the matter to [body] for further inquiry", "a coronial inquest into". **Apply the operational-endpoint tie-break (see below) when the recommendation chains a review with a downstream change.**
5. **Q5 — Does the recommendation specify a policy, protocol, guideline, SOP, escalation pathway, staff training programme, curriculum, education roll-out, or any other procedural change?**
 → **MECH-PROC**. Trigger phrases: "develop a [protocol]", "amend the [policy]", "the [SOP] should require", "review and update the [guideline]", "implement an escalation pathway", "officers be trained in", "deliver training in", "curriculum should include", "professional development", "competency be demonstrated". This is the residual procedural category and absorbs the largest share of recommendations.
6. **Q6 — None of the above.**
 → **MECH-OTHER**. Use for exhortations, observations, or vague calls to action that do not specify a mechanism ("I urge", "highlights the continued importance of", "stakeholders should consider this matter").

### The operational-endpoint tie-break (MECH-INV ↔ MECH-PROC) — promoted to its own rule in v2.1

The word "review" is overloaded and the recommendation may chain a *review* step with a *downstream change* step ("conduct a review and then amend the policy", "audit compliance and implement remediation"). v2.0 embedded the tie-break inside Q5/Q6; v2.1 promotes it to a top-level rule because three of the ten clean-subset mechanism disagreements on the v2.0 pilot were exactly this chained-verb pattern.

**The rule: follow the verb chain to the last operational verb.** Ask "when is the recommendation satisfied?":

- If the recommendation is satisfied **the moment the review report is delivered** (the report is the end-state) → **MECH-INV**.
- If the recommendation is **only** satisfied when the **downstream change** is implemented (the review is a step toward the change, not the deliverable) → **MECH-PROC**.

The endpoint is the *last operational verb in the chain*, not the first. "Review and amend the policy" — the endpoint verb is *amend* → MECH-PROC. "Review compliance with the policy" — the endpoint verb is *review* (the deliverable is the review) → MECH-INV.

#### Worked operational-endpoint examples

| Phrase | Endpoint verb | Code |
|---|---|---|
| "An audit / review should be undertaken **of compliance with** X" | review/audit (the audit is the deliverable) | MECH-INV |
| "An **independent review** of [program] should be conducted" | review (external inquiry; report is the deliverable) | MECH-INV |
| "Refer the matter to [body] for further inquiry" | inquire (inquiry is the endpoint) | MECH-INV |
| "A coronial inquest should be conducted into ..." | inquest (the inquest itself is the endpoint) | MECH-INV |
| "**Review and amend** the [policy]" | amend | MECH-PROC |
| "**Review** the [policy] **and update** it to reflect Y" | update | MECH-PROC |
| "**Review** the [policy/protocol] **to ensure** Y" | ensure (downstream change implied) | MECH-PROC |
| "X should **review** its [policy/practice]" | review/revise (in-house revision implied) | MECH-PROC |
| "**Examine whether** [policy] **needs amendment**" | amendment (downstream change) | MECH-PROC |
| "**Investigate** the matter **and implement** appropriate changes" | implement | MECH-PROC |

**Default for genuinely ambiguous "recommend a review of Y" with no further verb chain:** MECH-PROC if Y is a policy / protocol / practice (a review of these almost always implies revision); MECH-INV if Y is a program, agency, or events (a review of these is more often endpoint-as-report).

### Worked boundary examples (v2.1)

| Text fragment | Decision-tree path | Code |
|---|---|---|
| "Amend section 43 of the Health Act" | Q1 yes | MECH-LEG |
| "Replace the Taser system with a new model" | Q1 no → Q2 yes | MECH-INFRA |
| "Police officers be trained in de-escalation techniques" | Q1 no → Q2 no → Q3 no → Q4 no → Q5 yes (training is a procedural change in v2.1) | MECH-PROC |
| "Develop a head-injury protocol and ensure training is provided to officials" | Q1 no → Q2 no → Q3 no → Q4 no → Q5 yes (protocol + training; both are procedural) | MECH-PROC |

| "Allocate two additional psychiatric registrar positions" | Q1 no → Q2 no → Q3 yes | MECH-FUND |
| "Conduct an independent review of police pursuit policy" | Q1 no → Q2 no → Q3 no → Q4 yes (endpoint = review report) | MECH-INV |
| "Review and amend the police pursuit policy" | Q4 no (endpoint verb is *amend*) → Q5 yes | MECH-PROC |
| "Investigate the matter and implement appropriate changes" | Q4 no (endpoint verb is *implement*) → Q5 yes | MECH-PROC |
| "I urge the Department to take this matter seriously" | Q1..Q5 all no → Q6 | MECH-OTHER |

## Axis 3 — Addressee

*Who is being directed to act?* Code the **most-specific named addressee**. **No changes in v2.1.**

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| ADDR-DEPT-NAMED | Named government department | A named state or federal department, ministry, or agency, *including* state regulatory authorities that sit inside a department, *and* including state health authorities. |
| ADDR-DEPT-GENERIC | Government (generic) | "The government", "the State", unnamed. |
| ADDR-HEALTH | Health service / LHD / hospital | A specific health service, local health district, hospital, area health service, or operational care-delivery body. **Not** a state health department. |
| ADDR-REG | Independent regulator | A standalone statutory regulator that is not a department. |
| ADDR-PROF | Professional body / college | RACGP, ANMF, Law Society, AMA. |
| ADDR-POLICE | Police / corrections | A police force or corrections agency, regardless of its regulatory role. |
| ADDR-COURT | Coroner's court / judiciary | The coroner's court itself or other courts. |
| ADDR-PRIVATE | Private body | Named company, NGO, school, charity. |
| ADDR-UNSPEC | Unspecified | No clear addressee. |

### ADDR-HEALTH vs ADDR-DEPT-NAMED — the state-health-authority boundary (carried forward from v2.0)

**Apply this rule unconditionally:**

- **ADDR-DEPT-NAMED** = state or federal health department / authority / ministry. **All of these are DEPT-NAMED**:
 - NSW Health, Queensland Health, WA Health, SA Health, Department of Health (Victoria), Department of Health Tasmania, NT Health, ACT Health
 - The Department of Health and Aged Care (Commonwealth)
 - Local Health Networks (SA), Hospital and Health Services (Qld) when used to mean the state-level body
 - Any state Minister for Health
- **ADDR-HEALTH** = specific operational care-delivery body **below** the state level. All of these are HEALTH:
 - A named Local Health District / LHD (Sydney LHD, Hunter New England LHD)
 - A named hospital (Royal Prince Alfred Hospital, "the Hospital")
 - A named area health service (Northern Sydney Area Health Service)
 - A named community health centre, clinic, or operational health unit
 - A named ambulance service (Ambulance Tasmania, NSW Ambulance) — operational delivery body, not the department

**Boundary test.** "Could a Minister for Health direct this entity?" → DEPT-NAMED. "Is this an operational care-delivery body that delivers patient care?" → HEALTH.

### REG vs DEPT-NAMED (carried forward from v1.1)

- **ADDR-REG** = independent statutory regulators whose head reports to a board or parliament, not to a department secretary. Examples: AHPRA, TGA, ASIC, the Australian Health Care Safety and Quality Commission, the Coroner's Court itself, ACMA.
- **ADDR-DEPT-NAMED** = departments and agencies that sit inside the executive, even if they exercise regulatory functions. Examples: SafeWork NSW; EPA NSW; Australian Border Force; Office of the Public Advocate.
- Boundary check: if you can name the regulator's "minister responsible", it's almost certainly DEPT-NAMED.

### POLICE vs DEPT-NAMED (carried forward from v1.2)

- **ADDR-POLICE** = the operational police or corrections entity itself: the force name (NSW Police, AFP); the Commissioner of Police; a named station; Corrective Services NSW.
- **ADDR-DEPT-NAMED** = the political department overseeing a police force: Department of Communities and Justice; Department of Justice; Minister for Police.
- **Heuristic.** Operational executor → POLICE. Direction-only → DEPT-NAMED.

## Axis 4 — Specificity (binary, carried forward from v2.0)

*How actionable is the recommendation?* **Two-point categorical scale.** **No changes in v2.1.**

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| SPEC-2 | Specific | Named actor AND operational verb. Whether or not a deadline cue or measurable metric is present. |
| SPEC-1 | Aspirational | Missing named actor, OR aspirational verb, OR purely exhortative. |

### Two-part test

1. **Is there a named actor?** A named actor is a specific entity capable of being held accountable: a named body (NSW Health, the Public Advocate), a named hospital, a named regulator, a named professional college. The general public, "the medical profession", "those in [industry]", "stakeholders", or "the [thing] should be ..." (the thing is the object, not the actor) are **not** named actors.
2. **Does the verb specify an operational action?** Operational verbs: review, develop, implement, install, amend, introduce, replace, supply, fund, allocate, audit, undertake, conduct, issue, mandate, require, prohibit, restrict, train. Aspirational verbs (downgrade to SPEC-1): consider, explore, encourage, ensure (without specifics), bear in mind, take into account, recognise, acknowledge, work towards, strive for, needs to, should be aware, ought to be considered, be mindful of, prioritise (without operational target), focus on.

**Both yes → SPEC-2. Either no → SPEC-1.**

### Tie-break rules

- "**Should consider**" / "**is encouraged to**" → SPEC-1 (aspirational verb), even if the rest of the recommendation is specific. Exception: "consider working with [named body] to develop [deliverable]" → SPEC-2 (the deliverable carries the specificity).
- A recommendation that names *only the action* without an actor is SPEC-1.

### The `has_deadline_or_metric` boolean flag

For each row coded SPEC-2, set the boolean flag `has_deadline_or_metric = True` if the recommendation contains **any** of:

- **A deadline cue.** Explicit calendar date ("by 1 July 2027"); relative time-bound phrase ("within 30 days"); external deadline ("within the completion dates set out in the report"); scheduled event ("at the next meeting of [body, scheduled for date]"); external regulatory deadline.
- **A measurable outcome / target metric.** Numeric target ("reduce wait times below 30 minutes", "all officers" = ≥95% implied); binary verifiable state ("all cells fitted with CCTV"); defined process completion ("until competency is demonstrated").

Set the flag `False` otherwise. SPEC-1 rows take `has_deadline_or_metric = False` by default.

### Worked examples (real corpus)

| Text fragment | SPEC code | `has_deadline_or_metric` | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I recommend that Ambulance Tasmania implement the recommendations contained in the RCA report within the completion dates set out in the report" | SPEC-2 | True | Named actor + operational verb + external deadline |
| "I recommend that the Weapons Act 1990 (Qld) be amended to require that persons seeking to possess a gel blaster ... hold a valid Queensland Weapons License" | SPEC-2 | False | Named statute + amendment, no deadline / metric |
| "That the Office of Sport consider working with NSW Karate to develop a general head injury/concussion protocol" | SPEC-2 | False | Named actor + deliverable; "consider working ... to develop" carries SPEC-2 (the exception) |
| "The CIMHA system should be reviewed and optimised" | SPEC-1 | False | No named actor — "the system" is the object, not the actor |
| "The policy regarding the reporting of neonatal deaths needs to be clarified. All staff need to be aware of the policy" | SPEC-1 | False | Aspirational verbs; no named actor for the operational action |
| "Those in the piling industry adopt safe work method practices" | SPEC-1 | False | "Those in [industry]" is a class, not a named actor |
| "Greater coordination across agencies is needed" | SPEC-1 | False | Goal without bound actor |
| "I urge X to take this matter seriously" | SPEC-1 | False | Exhortative |
| "All cells in [Facility] to be fitted with CCTV by 31 December 2027" | SPEC-2 | True | Named actor (the facility operator) + binary verifiable state + calendar deadline |

## Axis 5 — Scope

*Is the recommendation addressing the individual case or proposing systemic reform?* **No changes in v2.1.**

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| SCOPE-IND | Individual | Action localised to a single facility, patient, or family. |
| SCOPE-SYS | Systemic | Action intended to apply across a population, sector, or jurisdiction. |
| SCOPE-MIX | Mixed | Genuine span of both — retained for inter-rater work where two coders may legitimately disagree on IND/SYS. |

### Decision rules

- A recommendation naming a single hospital, single facility, or single family → SCOPE-IND.
- A recommendation that names a single hospital but **explicitly cites systemic relevance** ("at SJOG Murdoch and across other facilities in the state") → SCOPE-SYS.
- A recommendation directed at "all [class]" or to a department covering a sector → SCOPE-SYS.

Scope was 95.5% raw agreement on the v2.0 clean-subset (n=44, α=0.872). The metric to read for this axis is **percent agreement**; α should be interpreted in the context of the modal share (≈80% SCOPE-SYS on this corpus).

## Flags

In addition to the five axis labels, each row carries optional booleans:

- `ambiguous_<axis>` — coder was torn between two categories on this axis (60/40 = dominant; 51/49 = ambiguous).
- `has_deadline_or_metric` — Set on SPEC-2 rows; preserves the v1.x SPEC-3 signal as a separate analysable property.
- `requires_legal_expertise` — meaningful interpretation requires legal training.
- `non_english_or_redacted` — text contains substantial non-English content or redactions.

## Reading the IRR output

`make irr` produces two files: `outputs/tables/irr_summary.csv` (raw α) and `outputs/tables/irr_summary_clean.csv` (catch-all-stack rows excluded). The catch-all filter fires when **at least one rater** uses the full catch-all stack (`THEME-OTHER` + `MECH-OTHER` + `ADDR-UNSPEC` + `SPEC-1` + `SCOPE-IND`) — corrected in v2.1's analysis script after the v2.0 re-analysis revealed asymmetric leak detection between raters (one rater treating a borderline row as no-recommendation while the other treats it as a real systemic recommendation). Both files include the % agreement column.

**Decision criterion for codebook revisions:** an axis is "Reliable" if **either** clean α ≥ 0.80 **or** (clean % agreement ≥ 90% AND the alpha shortfall is explained by one category dominating ≥ 90% of labels). The latter case is a marginal-skew artifact, not a codebook problem.

**For the binary Specificity axis,** % agreement is the more informative metric — α on a binary scale with one category at ≥85% will almost always be in the 0.4–0.6 band even when raw agreement is ≥95%. The v2.1 success criterion for Specificity (carried forward from v2.0) is **% agreement ≥ 90%**.
