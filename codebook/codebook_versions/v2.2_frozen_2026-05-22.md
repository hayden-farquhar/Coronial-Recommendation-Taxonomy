# Coronial Recommendation Coding Guide — v2.2

**Status:** Working version, post v2.1 pilot. v2.2 is a **narrow LEG ↔ PROC boundary-rule revision** of v2.1, with no changes to any other axis and no category collapses or additions.

The v2.1 pilot succeeded at what it set out to do (the operational-endpoint tie-break eliminated MECH-INV ↔ MECH-PROC disagreements — zero on Pass 2) but the TRAIN → PROC collapse pushed MECH-PROC's modal share to 64% on the clean subset (n=43), and Krippendorff's α is famously punitive to skewed distributions. Raw agreement on mechanism only dropped from 77.3% (v2.0) to 74.4% (v2.1), but α dropped from 0.675 to 0.541 because expected-agreement-by-chance went up with PROC dominance. The skew is now structural and would deepen further under aggressive collapse.

The remaining v2.1 mechanism disagreements (11 of 43 clean rows) concentrate on two boundaries that v2.1's text did not sharpen:

| Pair | Count | Pattern |
|---|---:|---|
| MECH-LEG ↔ MECH-PROC | 4 | Code of Practice vs. Industry Standard / Guideline; "consider [regulatory action]" verb vs. contemplated action; mixed multi-rec sets |
| MECH-FUND ↔ MECH-PROC | 3 | Q3 "purely about allocation" qualifier fails when funding bundles with review/policy |
| MECH-INFRA ↔ others | 3 | Scattered singletons |
| **MECH-INV ↔ MECH-PROC** | **0** | **v2.1's operational-endpoint rule eliminated this** ✅ |

v2.2 targets only **LEG ↔ PROC** — the 4 disagreements with the clearest semantic root and the most generalisable fix. FUND ↔ PROC is held for a possible v2.3 (the Q3 "purely" qualifier needs replacing with an endpoint-based test, analogous to v2.1's INV/PROC fix).

**v2.1's other-axis degradations (addressee 0.812 → 0.773; scope 0.872 → 0.772) are not codebook problems — they're sample drift.** v2.1 made no changes to addressee or scope axes. The α drops are dominated by marginal-skew effects (scope still at 90.7% raw agreement on a binary axis where one category dominates 72% of labels) and by re-emergence of the v1.2-era POLICE ↔ DEPT-NAMED boundary on 2 rows in the new sample. The codebook content is unchanged; the test is whether they rebound on the v2.2 fresh sample.

**Unit of analysis** and **coding decision** rules are unchanged from v2.1.

## Axis 2 — Mechanism (decision tree, v2.2)

*What kind of action is the recommendation calling for?* **Six mutually-exclusive categories** (unchanged from v2.1).

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| MECH-LEG | Legislative / regulatory | Changing a law, regulation, or **statutory code of practice**. |
| MECH-INFRA | Infrastructure / equipment | Installing, replacing, or supplying physical infrastructure or technology. |
| MECH-FUND | Funding / resourcing | Allocating money, FTE, or positions, where allocation is the only action. |
| MECH-INV | Investigation / inquiry | An audit, review, or further inquiry that is itself the **endpoint** of the recommendation. |
| MECH-PROC | Procedural | Policy, protocol, guideline, SOP, escalation pathway, training programme, **non-statutory industry standard**, curriculum, or staff-education roll-out — anything procedural that is not one of the above. |
| MECH-OTHER | Other / exhortation | Falls outside all of the above. Includes exhortations and observations without a specific named mechanism. |

### Decision tree (Q1..Q6, unchanged from v2.1)

Ask each question in order. **Take the first "yes". Do not re-evaluate later questions.**

1. **Q1 — Does the recommendation require amending a statute, regulation, or statutory code of practice?** → **MECH-LEG**.
2. **Q2 — Does the recommendation require installing, replacing, or supplying physical equipment or infrastructure?** → **MECH-INFRA**.
3. **Q3 — Is the recommendation purely about allocating money, FTE, or positions, with no other operational action?** → **MECH-FUND**.
4. **Q4 — Is the recommendation an external audit, inquiry, or review **as an endpoint** (the deliverable is the review report itself, not a downstream change)?** → **MECH-INV**.
5. **Q5 — Does the recommendation specify a policy, protocol, guideline, SOP, escalation pathway, staff training programme, non-statutory industry standard, curriculum, or other procedural change?** → **MECH-PROC**.
6. **Q6 — None of the above.** → **MECH-OTHER**.

### The MECH-LEG ↔ MECH-PROC boundary — new top-level rule in v2.2

The four v2.1 pilot disagreements on this boundary fall into three patterns. v2.2 addresses each with explicit rules.

#### Pattern 1: Code of Practice vs. Industry Standard vs. Guideline

A document with "Standard" or "Code" in its title is NOT automatically LEG. The test is **statutory status**, not the document's title.

**MECH-LEG (statutory codes / regulatory standards):**
- Documents that have **statutory force** under a parent Act — they are made under regulation-making powers, their breach can be cited in legal proceedings, and amending them follows a regulatory process. Examples: Mobile Crane Code of Practice 2006 (Qld) (made under the *Work Health and Safety Act 2011* (Qld)); the National Construction Code; Building Codes / Building Regulations; WHS Codes of Practice approved under WHS Acts; statutory professional codes of practice (e.g., approved AHPRA codes of conduct that have force under the *Health Practitioner Regulation National Law*).
- **Trigger test:** Can you point to a parent Act that this document was made under, and would breach of the document be relevant in court? → LEG.

**MECH-PROC (industry standards, guidelines, internal codes):**
- Documents that are **industry guidance, not statute-derived** — including industry-published standards, regulator-published guidance documents that elaborate on regulations, and internal organisational codes / policies / SOPs. Examples: the "Piling Work & Foundation Engineering Sites – Industry Standard" (industry-published by WorkSafe Victoria as a guideline); EWP Industry Good Practice Guides; Australian Standards (AS/NZS series) when they are referenced as voluntary unless a regulation explicitly incorporates them; in-house clinical guidelines; State Coroner's Guidelines; internal police OPMs and SOPs.
- **Trigger test:** Is this document published as guidance / best-practice / industry information rather than as a regulatory instrument? → PROC.

**Boundary cases:** An Australian Standard (e.g., AS/NZS 4935:2009) **becomes LEG** the moment a regulation makes it mandatory ("the regulations should require AS/NZS 4935 to be incorporated as a mandatory standard"); until then it is PROC.

#### Pattern 2: "Consider [regulatory action]" — code by the contemplated mechanism if the contemplated action is the deliverable

A recommendation phrased as "consider [doing X regulatory thing]" is NOT automatically PROC just because the verb is "consider". The test is **what the recommendation would be satisfied by**.

- If the recommendation is satisfied **only** when the regulatory action is taken (or formally declined after deliberation) → code by the **contemplated mechanism**. "Consider banning X" — the deliverable is the ban decision (taken or rejected); the ban itself is LEG; → **MECH-LEG**. "Reconsider the scope of practice of EENs within aged care" — the deliverable is a scope-of-practice change (which requires regulatory amendment); → **MECH-LEG**.
- If the recommendation is satisfied **the moment the consideration is documented** without requiring action either way → **MECH-PROC**. "Consider whether further review is warranted" — the deliverable is the consideration document; the next step is potentially-procedural; → MECH-PROC.

**This rule applies symmetrically to all "consider X" recommendations**, not only to regulatory ones. It is a sharpening of the v2.1 operational-endpoint tie-break (which was originally written for INV ↔ PROC) extended to the LEG ↔ PROC boundary: **follow the verb chain to the last operational verb, and code by the operational endpoint, not by the leading "consider"/"explore"/"review" verb.**

**Note on specificity (Axis 4) interaction:** SPEC-1 vs SPEC-2 is a separate axis. A "consider banning X" recommendation is **MECH-LEG and SPEC-1** simultaneously — LEG by the operational-endpoint rule applied to mechanism, SPEC-1 by the v2.1 "should consider" tie-break applied to specificity. Do not let the SPEC-1 coding leak into the mechanism coding.

#### Pattern 3: Mixed multi-rec sets

When a single text contains multiple distinct recommendations with different mechanisms (e.g., one LEG + one PROC), the codebook's "code by dominant" rule has been applied inconsistently — raters split on what counts as "dominant" (count? word count? coroner's first-stated?).

**v2.2 rule: code by the FIRST stated recommendation in the rec block.** Set `ambiguous_mechanism = TRUE` if the first-stated and second-stated recommendations are mechanistically distinct.

**Rationale:** "First stated" is the only fully-objective tie-break. Word count varies with detail level, not with importance. Count varies with how the coroner formats the recommendations. Coding by first-stated is reproducible across coders, even when the underlying judgement is genuinely contested.

**Apply this rule consistently across all axes that exhibit multi-rec sets** (mechanism, addressee, scope) — it is the most reliable tie-break when the codebook says "choose the dominant".

### Worked LEG ↔ PROC examples (drawn from v2.1 pilot disagreements)

| Text fragment | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "WorkSafe Victoria consult with the Piling and Foundation Safety Federation with the view to updating the 'Piling Work & Foundation Engineering Sites – Industry Standard'" | MECH-PROC | Industry Standard is industry-published guidance, not statutory. |
| "the National Transport Commission and Austroads be requested to amend the next version of Assessing Fitness to Drive" | MECH-LEG | Assessing Fitness to Drive is incorporated by reference into state licensing regulations — has regulatory force. |
| "Elevated Work Platforms... incorporated into the definition of a mobile crane so that the relevant provisions in Mobile Crane Code of Practice 2006 (Qld) apply" | MECH-LEG | Mobile Crane Code of Practice is a statutory code under the WHS Act (Qld). |
| "The Commonwealth Government reconsider the scope of practice of EENs within aged care, to increase and align the scope of practice" | MECH-LEG | Operational endpoint = scope of practice change (requires AHPRA regulatory amendment). The "reconsider" verb is the starting point, not the endpoint. |
| "the question of whether the sale of baby bath seats should be banned... should be considered nationally by the relevant regulatory agencies" | MECH-LEG | Contemplated action is a product ban (regulatory). The consideration is the path; the ban is the operational endpoint. |
| "the relevant regulatory agencies... a strong public awareness campaign should be instituted" | MECH-PROC | Awareness campaign is procedural; no regulatory amendment contemplated. |
| Rec block: "Local Governments consider implementing public awareness process" + "Building Commission consider phasing out child-resistant doors as barriers" | MECH-PROC, `ambiguous_mechanism=TRUE` | First-stated rec is procedural (public awareness). The Building Commission rec is LEG-adjacent (building code change). Code by first-stated; flag ambiguous. |
| "I urge the Department to take this matter seriously" | MECH-OTHER | Pure exhortation; Q1-Q5 all no. |

### Operational-endpoint tie-break for MECH-INV ↔ MECH-PROC (carried forward from v2.1)

The v2.1 rule eliminated INV/PROC disagreements on the v2.1 pilot (0 of 43) and is carried into v2.2 unchanged.

**Follow the verb chain to the last operational verb.** "Review and amend the policy" → MECH-PROC (endpoint = amend). "Audit compliance with the policy" → MECH-INV (endpoint = audit report).

## Axis 5 — Scope

*Unchanged from v2.1.*

Scope's v2.1 α (0.772) was sample-drift; the underlying signal is intact — 4 disagreements of 43 rows is 9.3% disagreement, 90.7% raw agreement on a binary axis. The α reads low because of the 72% modal-share skew, which is intrinsic to the corpus (≈80% of recommendations across Australian coronial inquests are systemic, not individual). The codebook reading rule (skew-adjusted Reliable when % agreement ≥ 90% AND modal share ≥ 90%) doesn't quite fire here (72% < 90% modal share), but the underlying disagreement count is low enough that a fresh sample is the right next test, not a codebook revision.

**For multi-rec sets with mixed individual/systemic scope:** code by the scope of the first-stated recommendation; set `ambiguous_scope=TRUE` if subsequent recs differ.

## Reading the IRR output

*Unchanged from v2.1.* `make irr` produces raw and clean α; the clean filter fires when at least one rater used the catch-all stack.

**Decision criterion for v2.2:** an axis is "Reliable" if **either** clean α ≥ 0.80 **or** (% agreement ≥ 90% AND modal share ≥ 90%).

**v2.2 success threshold for the mechanism axis specifically:** clean α ≥ 0.75 with PROC modal share ≤ 65% (some headroom). If PROC modal share has climbed further (say ≥ 70%) but raw agreement is ≥ 85%, treat as skew-adjusted Reliable and proceed to second-human-rater recruitment. If neither threshold is met, v2.3 will sharpen FUND ↔ PROC with an endpoint-based test analogous to the INV ↔ PROC rule that succeeded in v2.1.

**Success criterion for the v2.2 pilot:** mechanism reaches clean α ≥ 0.75 (or skew-adjusted Reliable via 85%+ agreement at 70%+ modal share). Other four axes maintain Reliable from v2.0/v2.1 baselines (theme 0.84+, addressee 0.80+, scope 90%+ agreement, specificity 0.85+).

If mechanism fails the v2.2 threshold, **v2.3 plan**: replace Q3 ("purely about allocating money, FTE, or positions") with an endpoint-based test ("Q3 — Is the recommendation's operational endpoint allocating money, FTE, or positions?"). This is the FUND ↔ PROC analogue of the v2.1 INV ↔ PROC fix. Held in reserve only.

**The aggressive v3.0 redesign (collapse FUND + INV into PROC, leaving LEG / INFRA / PROC / OTHER) is no longer the assumed next step.** Diagnosis: the v2.1 α drop was largely a Krippendorff-skew artefact from PROC dominance, and further collapse would deepen that skew (predicted PROC modal share ≈ 76% post-v3.0-collapse). Targeted boundary-rule fixes (v2.2, v2.3) are the lower-skew alternative. v3.0 collapse only justified if v2.3 also fails.
