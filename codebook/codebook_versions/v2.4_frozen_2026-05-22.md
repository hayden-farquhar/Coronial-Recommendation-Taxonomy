# Coronial Recommendation Coding Guide — v2.4

**Status:** Working version, post v2.3 human pilot. v2.4 is a **specificity-only revision** of v2.3 that resolves the persistent SPEC-1 ↔ SPEC-2 boundary ambiguity that survived three earlier iterations (v1.2 → v2.0 → v2.3). On the v2.3 human pilot (Hayden vs Opus, n=43 + n=39 clean), specificity α sat at 0.40 (v2.3 sample) / 0.29 (v2.2 sample) with 20 disagreements across both samples, **16 of which were asymmetric in one direction** (Human=SPEC-1 / Opus=SPEC-2). The asymmetry signals genuine codebook ambiguity, not random noise: human and LLM coders applied different default thresholds to the v2.3 "consider [verb chain]" exception rule and to the SPEC-2 named-actor test.

v2.4 makes two changes to the specificity axis only. No other axis changes.

## Axis 4 — Specificity (revised in v2.4)

*How actionable is the recommendation?* **Two-point categorical scale (unchanged categories; revised rules).**

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| SPEC-2 | Specific | Named, accountable, sub-jurisdictional actor AND operational verb whose lead is NOT "consider" / "give consideration to" / "should consider". |
| SPEC-1 | Aspirational | Missing named-accountable actor, OR lead verb is "consider" / "give consideration to" / "should consider" / "explore" / "encourage" / aspirational verb, OR purely exhortative. |

### v2.4 change (a) — "Consider [verb chain]" exception ELIMINATED

The v2.3 codebook had an exception: "consider [verb chain]" fires SPEC-2 when paired with a "specifiable downstream deliverable" (form, protocol, training programme, register, equipment item). This exception was the dominant driver of human-LLM disagreement on the v2.3 pilot — coders applied different default thresholds for what counted as "specifiable enough".

**v2.4 rule: there is no exception.** Any recommendation whose **operative lead verb** is `consider`, `give consideration to`, `should consider`, `explore`, `encourage`, `bear in mind`, `take into account`, `recognise`, `acknowledge`, `work towards`, `strive for`, `prioritise` (without an operational object), or any other aspirational verb → **SPEC-1**, regardless of what follows.

**Rationale:** A recommendation that says "consider [X]" only commits the recipient to consideration. It does not commit them to do X, even if X is well-specified. The recipient can defensibly comply by deliberating and declining. SPEC-2 should be reserved for recommendations whose recipient must take operational action to comply.

**Worked examples (all SPEC-1 under v2.4):**

| Text | v2.3 ruling | v2.4 ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Office of Sport consider working with NSW Karate to develop a head-injury protocol" | SPEC-2 (exception fires) | **SPEC-1** | Lead verb is "consider"; SPEC-2 needs an operational lead verb |
| "Minister consider implementing a scheme for short-form neurosurgical skills training for general surgeons" | SPEC-2 | **SPEC-1** | "Consider implementing" — consider is the operative ask |
| "Maritime Safety Victoria consider advising boat users in pamphlets" | SPEC-2 | **SPEC-1** | "Consider advising" |
| "Department of Health consider whether further review is warranted" | SPEC-1 | **SPEC-1** | Unchanged |
| "Department of Health review and update the Inhalant Abuse booklet" | SPEC-2 (no "consider") | **SPEC-2** | Operational lead verb |
| "The TGA consider whether products containing sodium nitrate should be restricted" | SPEC-2 (exception applied) | **SPEC-1** | "Consider whether" — pure deliberation |
| "I recommend banning the sale of X" | SPEC-2 | **SPEC-2** | Direct operational ask |
| "I recommend that the Minister give consideration to introducing standards for X" | SPEC-2 (exception) | **SPEC-1** | "Give consideration" |

### v2.4 change (b) — SPEC-2 named-actor test sharpened

The v2.3 codebook said role labels ("the relevant department", "those responsible") fail the named-actor test → SPEC-1. v2.4 extends this: **jurisdictional shorthand** (an entity named only at the level of jurisdiction without identifying a specific department, body, or agency) also fails the SPEC-2 named-actor test.

**Fail (SPEC-1):**
- "the ACT Government" (jurisdiction without department)
- "the Commonwealth Government" (jurisdiction without department)
- "the State Government" (generic + jurisdictional)
- "the NSW Government" (jurisdiction without department)
- "the Australian Government" (jurisdictional)
- "the government" (generic)
- "the relevant department", "the appropriate authorities", "those responsible" (carried over from v2.3)

**Pass (SPEC-2-eligible):**
- "the ACT Department of Health" — named department within ACT Government
- "the Australian Department of Health and Aged Care" — named department within Commonwealth Government
- "the Minister for Police in NSW" — specifically named office within NSW Government
- "the Coroners Court of NSW" — specifically named body
- "Royal Darwin Hospital" — specifically named facility
- "AHPRA" — specifically named regulator
- "WorkSafe Victoria" — specifically named body within Victorian Government
- "VicRoads" — specifically named agency

**Note on the asymmetry with addressee axis:** This SPEC-2 named-actor test is **stricter** than the ADDR-DEPT-NAMED addressee test. "ACT Government" can be coded ADDR-DEPT-NAMED (jurisdictional shorthand for a named state/territory government) but FAILS the SPEC-2 named-actor test (no specifically-named department to be held accountable).

The asymmetry is intentional. The addressee axis answers "who is this directed at?" — a coronial direction to "the ACT Government" identifies the jurisdiction. The specificity axis answers "how actionable is this?" — actionability requires identifying who within that jurisdiction is accountable for executing the recommendation. A direction to "the ACT Government" leaves accountability undefined; a direction to "the ACT Department of Health" specifies it.

### Two-part test (revised)

A recommendation is **SPEC-2** if BOTH:

1. **Named, accountable, sub-jurisdictional actor.** The recommendation directs a specifically-named department, body, hospital, court, regulator, or college that can be cited and held accountable. Jurisdictional shorthand ("the X Government") and role labels ("the relevant department") fail.
2. **Operational lead verb.** The recommendation's primary verb is operational (review, develop, implement, install, amend, introduce, replace, supply, fund, allocate, audit, undertake, conduct, issue, mandate, require, prohibit, restrict, train). Aspirational lead verbs ("consider", "give consideration to", "should consider", "explore", "encourage", "work towards") fail.

If both pass → **SPEC-2**. If either fails → **SPEC-1**.

### The `has_deadline_or_metric` boolean flag

*Unchanged from v2.3.* For each row coded SPEC-2, set the boolean flag `has_deadline_or_metric = True` if the recommendation contains a deadline cue (calendar date, relative time-bound phrase, external deadline, scheduled event, regulatory deadline) OR a measurable outcome / target metric (numeric target, binary verifiable state, defined process completion). Otherwise False. SPEC-1 rows take False.

This flag carries the *fully-specified deliverable* signal that was previously the v2.3 exception driver — preserved as a separate analytic property without contaminating the categorical IRR.

### Worked examples (v2.4)

| Text fragment | SPEC code | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Royal Darwin Hospital should give consideration to backfilling the second triage nurse position" | SPEC-1 | "Give consideration" is aspirational lead verb |
| "Royal Darwin Hospital should backfill the second triage nurse position" | SPEC-2 | Named actor + operational lead verb ("backfill") |
| "The ACT Government amend section 43 of the Health Act 1993" | SPEC-1 | Operational lead verb but "ACT Government" is jurisdictional shorthand, not specifically-accountable |
| "The ACT Department of Health amend section 43 of the Health Act 1993" | SPEC-2 | Specifically-named department + operational verb |
| "The Department of Health review and update the Inhalant Abuse booklet" | SPEC-2 | Named department + operational verb (the booklet contents need not be itemised) |
| "Office of Sport consider working with NSW Karate to develop a head-injury protocol" | SPEC-1 | "Consider working" — aspirational lead |
| "Office of Sport work with NSW Karate to develop a head-injury protocol" | SPEC-2 | Operational lead ("work with") |
| "I urge the Department to take this matter seriously" | SPEC-1 | Exhortative |
| "All cells in [Facility] to be fitted with CCTV by 31 December 2027" | SPEC-2 | Named facility + operational verb + deadline (also `has_deadline_or_metric=True`) |

## Reading the IRR output

*Unchanged from v2.3.* Decision criterion for an axis being "Reliable": clean α ≥ 0.80, or skew-adjusted Reliable (% agreement ≥ 90% AND modal share ≥ 90%).

**v2.4 expectation for specificity:** the asymmetric pattern on the v2.3 pilot (16/20 disagreements in one direction) suggests v2.4's rule changes will lift specificity α substantially. Pre-v2.4 the human read SPEC-1 strictly; Opus applied the "consider" exception liberally. Under v2.4 the rule is unambiguous — all "consider" lead verbs → SPEC-1.
