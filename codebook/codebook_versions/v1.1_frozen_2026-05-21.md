# Coronial Recommendation Coding Guide — v1.1

**Status:** Working version, post v1.0 pilot. v1.1 adds explicit decision rules for the three axes that scored below the "Reliable" threshold (α ≥ 0.80) on the v1.0 clean-subset analysis: **specificity** (clean α 0.352), **addressee** (0.720), and **mechanism** (0.733). No category additions in v1.1 — the deficits were definitional, not coverage gaps.

**Unit of analysis:** A single recommendation as extracted from a coronial finding. A finding with three numbered recommendations contributes three rows.

**Coding decision:** Each recommendation receives one label per axis (single-label, not multi-label). If a recommendation appears to legitimately span two categories on an axis, choose the *dominant* one and flag the case with the `ambiguous_<axis>` boolean for later review.

---

## Axis 1 — Theme

*What is the recommendation about?* Twelve mutually-exclusive categories.

| Code | Label | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| THEME-MH | Mental health & suicide prevention | Screening, follow-up, crisis services, involuntary treatment, suicide prevention messaging. | "All EDs should have 24-hour psychiatric liaison." |
| THEME-CUST | Custody, detention, and police use of force | In-custody deaths (police, corrections, immigration); restraint techniques; Tasers; police-family interactions during fatal encounters. | "CCTV monitoring of all cells holding intoxicated detainees." "Review of Taser deployment protocols." |
| THEME-MED | Healthcare delivery | Clinical care, medication safety, hospital systems, ambulance services, vaccine information. | "Mandatory medication reconciliation on admission." |
| THEME-RTA | Road & transport safety | Road, rail, aviation, maritime *transport*. (Recreational water use is `THEME-REC`.) | "Lower speed limit on the Hume Highway between X and Y." |
| THEME-DV | Domestic & family violence | Intimate-partner violence response. | "Police to attend all reported breaches of ADVO." |
| THEME-DRUG | Substance use | Illicit drugs, alcohol, prescribing-related harm. | "Pill-testing pilots at music festivals." |
| THEME-CHILD | Child protection | Child welfare, out-of-home care, mandatory reporting. | "FACS to review case plan within 14 days of any reported absconding." |
| THEME-WORK | Workplace safety | Occupational health and safety. | "SafeWork NSW to issue guidance on confined-space entry." |
| THEME-REC | Recreational & water safety | Water sports, boating, swimming, river safety, drownings outside vehicles, beach safety, outdoor recreation. | "Develop public education programs about the dangers of inland river water-skiing." |
| THEME-FIRE | Fire & structural safety | Smoke detectors, building code, evacuation, residential fire risk, structural safety of dwellings. | "Smoke detectors should be mandatory in informal sleeping spaces." |
| THEME-PROD | Product, chemical & poison safety | Regulation of dangerous substances, consumer products, chemicals, poisons; product recalls. | "Restrict retail sale of concentrated potassium cyanide." |
| THEME-OTHER | Other / cross-cutting | Use only when none of the above fits. Flag for review. | (use sparingly) |

### Decision rules

- **Maritime transport (THEME-RTA) vs. recreational water (THEME-REC).** A commercial ferry sinking is RTA; a family in a tinnie capsizing while fishing is REC.
- **Custody (THEME-CUST) vs. mental health (THEME-MH).** If a death occurs in custody but the recommendation targets mental-health screening of detainees, prefer the *intervention* axis: a screening recommendation is MH, a cell-design recommendation is CUST.
- **Healthcare delivery (THEME-MED) vs. mental health (THEME-MH).** MED is for the *delivery system*; MH is for *psychiatric / suicide-specific* content. A general ED triage recommendation is MED; an ED psychiatric-liaison recommendation is MH.

## Axis 2 — Mechanism

*What kind of action is the recommendation calling for?*

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| MECH-LEG | Legislative / regulatory | Changing a law, regulation, code of practice. |
| MECH-PROC | Procedural | New or modified policy, protocol, guideline, SOP. |
| MECH-TRAIN | Training & education | Staff or public education. |
| MECH-FUND | Funding / resourcing | Additional staff, money, equipment. |
| MECH-INFRA | Infrastructure / equipment | Physical infrastructure or technology. |
| MECH-INV | Investigation / inquiry | A further inquiry, audit, or review. |
| MECH-OTHER | Other | Falls outside the above. Includes *exhortations* / observations without a specific named mechanism (e.g. "I urge X to ..." with no specific action). |

### Decision rules (new in v1.1)

**Precedence when more than one mechanism applies.** A single recommendation may *touch* training and policy and equipment. Code by the **operative** mechanism — the one without which the recommendation collapses. Tie-breaks in this order:

1. **MECH-LEG** wins if the operative action is amending a statute, regulation, or code of practice. Trigger phrases: "amend the Act", "the regulations should require", "regulation X should be replaced", "introduce legislation".
2. **MECH-INFRA** wins (over MECH-PROC) if the operative action is a physical thing being installed, replaced, or supplied. Trigger phrases: "install", "replace", "fit out", "supply", "purchase", "provide [device/equipment]".
3. **MECH-TRAIN** wins (over MECH-PROC) if the operative action is education or curriculum. Trigger phrases: "training programme", "curriculum should include", "be educated about", "professional development", "competency be demonstrated". A policy *requiring* training is still TRAIN.
4. **MECH-FUND** wins (over MECH-PROC) if the operative action is allocating money, positions, or hours. Trigger phrases: "additional funding", "increased staffing", "X FTE", "resource the service".
5. **MECH-INV** wins (over MECH-PROC) if the operative action is conducting an audit, review, or further inquiry — *not* policy revision arising from one. Trigger phrases: "an audit should be undertaken", "review by an independent body", "a coronial inquest into...".
6. **MECH-PROC** is the residue: policies, protocols, guidelines, SOPs, decision-support tools, escalation pathways — anything procedural where none of (1)-(5) is the operative action.
7. **MECH-OTHER** for exhortations / observations with no specific named action ("I urge", "highlights the continued importance of").

### Worked boundary examples

| Text fragment | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Develop a head-injury protocol for use at gradings" | MECH-PROC | Operative action is a protocol, not the training that follows from it |
| "Develop a head-injury protocol and provide training to officials" | MECH-TRAIN | Training is named as the operative outcome; the protocol is the substrate |
| "Replace the Taser system with a new model" | MECH-INFRA | Replace equipment is physical; not procedural |
| "Update the Taser deployment policy" | MECH-PROC | Policy is the operative action |
| "Amend section 43 of the Health Act" | MECH-LEG | Statutory change |
| "Allocate two additional psychiatric registrar positions" | MECH-FUND | Allocation is the operative action |

## Axis 3 — Addressee

*Who is being directed to act?* Code the **most-specific named addressee**.

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| ADDR-DEPT-NAMED | Named government department | A named state or federal department, ministry, or agency, *including* state regulatory authorities that sit inside a department. |
| ADDR-DEPT-GENERIC | Government (generic) | "The government", "the State", unnamed. |
| ADDR-HEALTH | Health service / LHD / hospital | A named health service, local health district, or hospital. |
| ADDR-REG | Independent regulator | A standalone statutory regulator that is not a department. |
| ADDR-PROF | Professional body / college | RACGP, ANMF, Law Society, AMA. |
| ADDR-POLICE | Police / corrections | A police force or corrections agency, regardless of its regulatory role. |
| ADDR-COURT | Coroner's court / judiciary | The coroner's court itself or other courts. |
| ADDR-PRIVATE | Private body | Named company, NGO, school, charity. |
| ADDR-UNSPEC | Unspecified | No clear addressee. |

### Decision rules (new in v1.1)

**The REG vs DEPT-NAMED boundary** was the dominant source of disagreement on the v1.0 pilot. Use this rule:

- **ADDR-REG** is reserved for **independent statutory regulators** — bodies whose head reports to a board or parliament, not to a department secretary. Examples: AHPRA, TGA, ASIC, AustralianHealthcareSafetyandQualityCommission, the Coroner's Court itself, ACMA.
- **ADDR-DEPT-NAMED** for **departments and agencies that sit inside the executive**, even if they exercise regulatory functions. Examples: SafeWork NSW (sits inside DCJ); EPA NSW (statutory authority but tied to the Ministry); NSW Health; Department of Health and Aged Care; Australian Border Force; Office of the Public Advocate.
- **ADDR-POLICE** for police forces and corrections agencies. Use this code even if the recommendation concerns their regulatory or licensing functions — do not split police into REG.
- Boundary check: if you can name the regulator's "minister responsible", it's almost certainly DEPT-NAMED. Independent statutory regulators report to parliament, not a minister.

### Worked examples

| Addressee mentioned | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "AHPRA" | ADDR-REG | Independent statutory regulator |
| "TGA" | ADDR-REG | Independent statutory regulator |
| "SafeWork NSW" | ADDR-DEPT-NAMED | Sits inside Department of Customer Service |
| "EPA NSW" | ADDR-DEPT-NAMED | Statutory authority within the Ministry of the Environment |
| "NSW Police" | ADDR-POLICE | Police force, period — even when discussing licensing |
| "Corrective Services NSW" | ADDR-POLICE | Corrections — same rule |
| "Coroner's Court of NSW" | ADDR-COURT | Court |
| "The Public Advocate" | ADDR-DEPT-NAMED | Office sits inside the executive |
| "RACGP" | ADDR-PROF | Professional college |
| "The State" / "the Government" | ADDR-DEPT-GENERIC | Unnamed |

## Axis 4 — Specificity

*How actionable is the recommendation?* A 3-point ordinal scale.

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| SPEC-3 | Specific & measurable | Specific action AND named actor AND **either** a deadline cue **or** a measurable outcome / target metric (disjunction — either one is sufficient). |
| SPEC-2 | Specific (no metric or date) | Specific action AND named actor but no deadline cue or outcome metric. |
| SPEC-1 | Aspirational | Names a goal or principle without binding any actor or action, OR is purely exhortative. |

### Decision rules (new in v1.1)

**This axis had the largest reliability collapse (clean α 0.352).** All three v1.0 disagreements involved **missed deadline cues** — one rater catching a date or completion-timeframe phrase, the other not. v1.1 lists the cues explicitly.

**A deadline cue is *any* of:**

- **An explicit calendar date.** "By 1 July 2027", "before 31 December", "by mid-2026", "from the start of the next financial year".
- **A relative time-bound phrase.** "Within 30 days", "within six months", "within the next year", "no later than ...".
- **A reference to an external deadline.** "Within the completion dates set out in the [named] report", "by the conclusion of the [named] review", "in time for the next [recurring] meeting".
- **A scheduled event.** "At the next meeting of [body, scheduled for date]", "before the next session of parliament".
- **An external regulatory deadline.** "In time for the [Act] reforms commencing on [date]".

**A measurable outcome / target metric is *any* of:**

- A numeric target. "Reduce wait times below 30 minutes", "increase coverage to 95%", "all officers" (≥ 95% implied).
- A binary verifiable state. "All cells fitted with CCTV", "every shift to have a registered nurse".
- A defined process completion. "Until competency is demonstrated", "until accreditation is achieved".

**A specific action is *any* of:** review, develop, implement, install, amend, introduce, replace, supply, fund, allocate, audit, undertake, conduct, issue, mandate, require, prohibit, restrict, train.

**An aspirational verb (SPEC-1 only) is *any* of:** consider, explore, encourage, ensure (without specifics), bear in mind, take into account, recognise, acknowledge, work towards, strive for.

### Worked examples (real corpus)

| Text fragment | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I recommend that Ambulance Tasmania implement the recommendations contained in the RCA report **within the completion dates set out in the report**" | SPEC-3 | Named actor + specific action + external-deadline cue |
| "I recommend tabling for consideration at the next National Emergency Communications Working Group (**scheduled for 23 May 2018**) the following agenda item..." | SPEC-3 | Named actors + specific action + calendar date |
| "I recommend that the Weapons Act 1990 (Qld) be amended to require that persons seeking to possess a gel blaster ... hold a valid Queensland Weapons License" | SPEC-2 | Specific action + named statute, no deadline / metric |
| "That the Office of Sport consider working with NSW Karate to develop a general head injury/concussion protocol" | SPEC-2 | Named actor + specific action, no deadline. Note: "consider" alone would be SPEC-1, but "consider working ... to develop" names a deliverable |
| "Greater coordination across agencies is needed" | SPEC-1 | Goal without bound actor or action |
| "I urge X to take this matter seriously" | SPEC-1 | Exhortative |

### Tie-break rules

- "**Should consider**" / "**is encouraged to**" lowers SPEC by one band relative to "shall" / "must" — but only if the rest of the recommendation is otherwise specific.
- A recommendation that quotes a **deadline already in legislation** and re-applies it does count as a deadline for SPEC-3.
- A recommendation that names *only the action* without an actor is SPEC-1 (no named actor to be held accountable).
- If a recommendation has a date cue AND a metric, still SPEC-3 (no SPEC-4).

## Axis 5 — Scope

*Is the recommendation addressing the individual case or proposing systemic reform?*

| Code | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| SCOPE-IND | Individual | Action localised to a single facility, patient, or family. |
| SCOPE-SYS | Systemic | Action intended to apply across a population, sector, or jurisdiction. |
| SCOPE-MIX | Mixed | Genuine span of both — retained for inter-rater work where two coders may legitimately disagree on IND/SYS. |

---

## Flags

In addition to the five axis labels, each row carries optional booleans:

- `ambiguous_<axis>` — coder was torn between two categories on this axis (60/40 = dominant; 51/49 = ambiguous).
- `requires_legal_expertise` — meaningful interpretation requires legal training.
- `non_english_or_redacted` — text contains substantial non-English content or redactions.

## Appendix — Unlabelled real-corpus snippets

These five real recommendations are deliberately **unlabelled** so they calibrate coders to prose style without anchoring labels.

> *(NSW)* "1. That the Office of Sport consider working with NSW Karate (and possibly other martial arts organisations) to develop a general head injury/concussion protocol or policy for the use of karate organisations at gradings and training. This process should take place with expert medical advice."

> *(WA)* "I recommend telephone calls recorded prior to the death of a prisoner in custody be provided to the coroners court as they were in the past. All prison deaths are mandated to be inquested, and while I appreciate not all telephone calls, although recorded are listened to within the prison system, nor is it feasible, they may provide some insight, post the event, as to what may have been in operation in the prisoner's mind at the time."

> *(ACT)* "I recommend that the ACT Government amend section 43 of the Health Act 1993 to avoid a repetition of this conduct."

> *(SA)* "Pursuant to Section 25(2) of the Coroner's Act 1975, I recommend that General Medical Practitioners be reminded that, when a patient has been referred to a specialist, it is necessary to communicate clearly with that specialist before prescribing medication or providing other forms of treatment which may be relevant to treatment being provided by the specialist."

> *(VIC)* "Pursuant to section 72(2) of the Coroners Act 2008, I make the following recommendation(s) connected with the death: 1. That the Public Advocate review OPA policy in relation to the scope of exercise of guardianship powers in particular in relation to the level of supervision and follow up of delivery of medical treatment."
